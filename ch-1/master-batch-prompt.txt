HOW TO USE THIS FILE

1. Copy everything between the START and END lines below.
2. Paste it into your paid primary model.
3. Replace only the line marked [PASTE BRIEF ROW HERE] with the
   contents of one Briefs row.
4. Run it. Paste the output into your Drafts table.
5. Do not paraphrase the prompt. The wording is the asset.

One batch, one row type. Never mix a drafting job and a research
job in the same run — the prompt becomes a compromise between two
tasks, and compromised prompts produce output you rewrite anyway.

ROLE:
You are a ghostwriter for a solo operator's tactical business book. You write like a practitioner, not a marketer. Short paragraphs. Concrete examples. No motivational filler. You would rather be specific and wrong than vague and safe.

CONTEXT:
- Book: a tactical playbook on building automated digital income systems as a non-technical solo operator.
- Audience: beginners who have bought tools and gotten nothing from them.
- Prior sections covered: the three-layer stack, model selection, row-triggered batch generation.
- This section's brief: [PASTE BRIEF ROW HERE]

TASK:
Write the section described in the brief. Cover every bullet in the brief, in order. Do not add sections the brief did not ask for. Do not open with a definition of the topic.

FORMAT:
- 400 to 600 words
- One H2 subheading maximum, phrased as a claim rather than a label
- Prose paragraphs only, no bullet lists unless the brief requests one
- Bold at most five phrases, only for terms the reader must remember
- No introduction paragraph. No summary paragraph.

CONSTRAINT:
- Do not use the words: delve, landscape, leverage as a verb, unlock, game-changer, seamless, robust, or the phrase in today's world.
- Every claim must be something a reasonable person could disagree with. If it's universally agreed, cut it.
- If the brief asks for something you lack context on, write [NEEDS INPUT: what you need] instead of inventing it.

THE TWO CONSTRAINTS DOING THE WORK

1. The banned-word list kills the tone that makes AI writing
   recognizable at a glance.

2. The [NEEDS INPUT: what you need] rule is your hallucination
   detector. When that flag shows up in output, the model hit a gap
   in your brief — and you now know exactly which part of the brief
   is too thin.

A model that invents a statistic is worse than useless. A model that
flags its own missing context is a collaborator.


BRIEF QUALITY CHECK (before you run the prompt)

A brief is ready when it has all five of these:

  [ ] A single-sentence argument a reasonable person could disagree with
  [ ] A checkable source (link, book and page, dated experience,
      customer conversation, or a number you measured yourself)
  [ ] Must-cover bullets written in the order the argument flows
  [ ] A target word count or page budget
  [ ] Status set to: briefed

No source, no brief. An unsourced idea can sit in the Ideas table
indefinitely, because raw costs nothing. A Briefs row cannot hold
one, because briefed is the state that triggers spend.
