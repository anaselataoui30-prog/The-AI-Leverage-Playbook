HOW TO USE THIS FILE

Model three prices. Then choose using a metric most authors never
compute: revenue per visitor.

Revenue per sale is the number that feels relevant and the number
that misleads. Revenue per visitor is the honest one: price
multiplied by the share of people who buy at that price.

Run all three tiers against a real week of traffic, not a guess. If
you have no traffic yet, ship at core, then test entry for two weeks
once Chapter 5 sends people to the page. A price tested against zero
visitors is a preference, not a decision.

THE WORD THAT MATTERS IS "WHOLE."

The rate applies to the entire price, not to the portion above or
below a line. So when you cross the boundary, every dollar of your
price changes rate at once, not just the last one. Moving a price by
one dollar in the wrong direction can cut your revenue per sale
nearly in half, because you did not lose a dollar, you lost the rate.

The danger is not the number. It is that the number is a cliff
rather than a slope, and cliffs do not warn you.

CONVERSION RISK, per tier:

  Entry    Low price reads as low value and attracts buyers who
           want a refund more than an outcome.
  Core     The safe default. Under-pricing here quietly donates
           margin you will never recover.
  Premium  Conversion drops sharply and does not recover without
           proof elements in the description.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
WORKED SHAPE (structure only — no rates)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

This example shows how the arithmetic is laid out. The rate, the
cost, and the conversion numbers are placeholders, not advice.
Substitute your Step 0 figures.

  Entry   price $A    rate R1    cost $c1    visitors 400
          conversions 16   →  4.0%   →  RPV = ($A x R1 - $c1) x 0.04
  Core    price $B    rate R2    cost $c2    visitors 400
          conversions 8    →  2.0%   →  RPV = ($B x R2 - $c2) x 0.02
  Premium price $C    rate R3    cost $c3    visitors 400
          conversions 3    →  0.75%  →  RPV = ($C x R3 - $c3) x 0.0075

Do not compare the prices. Compare the RPV column. That is the
whole point of the model.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
TWO CONSEQUENCES TO CARRY INTO THE LISTING
---------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. Never set a price from memory. Open the current terms and
     re-derive it each launch. Terms change; your memory of them
     does not.

  2. Never assume lower price means more sales volume compensates.
     A discounted price also signals a discounted product to the
     exact buyer who was comparing you to nothing.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
PAPERBACK — THE MATH THAT CATCHES PEOPLE
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Ebook revenue is a rate applied to a price with a delivery cost
subtracted. Print revenue is a rate applied to a price with a
printing cost subtracted, and the printing cost scales with page
count and format in a way file size does not.

Thicker means more expensive to produce, and that cost comes out
before your share.

  [ ] Page count and format recorded in Step 0
  [ ] Printing cost for that exact page count written down
  [ ] Price was NOT set by adding a fixed amount to the ebook price
      — that is how people accidentally sell at a loss

The reason to do the paperback anyway is trust. A buyer comparing
two unknown authors will treat the one with a paperback as the more
established of the two, regardless of whether it is true. That is a
cheap credibility layer, and it is the entire justification.
