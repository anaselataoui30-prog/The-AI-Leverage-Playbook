HOW TO USE THIS FILE

Three elements do the work, and all three operate under a size
constraint. Metadata is not decoration on the listing — it is the
distribution.

A listing is not a storefront you decorate. It is an interception
point, placed where a specific person is already typing a specific
phrase with a card already out.

Not "how do I look professional" but "which search am I the best
available answer to."

---------------------------------------------------------------------

  [ ] Title is readable when the image is the width of a search
      result
  [ ] Subtitle is legible, but secondary to the title
  [ ] One idea per cover
  [ ] No thin typefaces, no low contrast
  [ ] Title is not smaller than the author name
  [ ] Tested small before tested pretty

If the title is unreadable at thumbnail width, the cover has failed
at its only job. Check it at thumbnail size first, every time.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
ELEMENT 2 — THE THREE-LINE DESCRIPTION
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Most of the description is collapsed behind a read-more. The buyer
decides on the three lines they can see, and those lines must
restate the promise: outcome, audience, timeframe, obstacle.

THE BUILDER

  LINE 1 — OUTCOME (the change of state, imperative or declarative)
  ______________________________________________________________

  LINE 2 — AUDIENCE (who it is for, including the situation they
  are already in)
  ______________________________________________________________

  LINE 3 — TIMEFRAME + OBSTACLE (a number, and the thing they
  already tried and failed at)
  ______________________________________________________________

THE TEST — do these three lines read before the read-more fold?

  [ ] Outcome is in line 1, not line 3
  [ ] Audience names a person who recognises themselves
  [ ] Timeframe is a number
  [ ] Obstacle names something they already tried
  [ ] Nothing in the first three lines describes the book —
      they describe the reader and the outcome

WEAK OPENING
  "This comprehensive guide explores the fascinating relationship
  between artificial intelligence and modern content creation,
  offering readers a deep dive into the tools and techniques
  reshaping the publishing industry."

STRONG OPENING
  "Write one grounded chapter a day. This 40-page playbook is for
  non-technical solo operators who have bought AI tools and gotten
  nothing from them. Publish your first product in 30 days without
  hiring a writer or writing a line of code."

The weak version describes the book. The strong version describes
the reader and the outcome. A buyer cannot evaluate a book, but they
can recognise themselves.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
ELEMENT 3 — THE SEVEN KEYWORD SLOTS
---------------------------------------------------------------------

These hold buyer phrases, not author vocabulary. This is where most
listings leak discovery.

  AN AUTHOR TERM is how you describe your own work.
  A BUYER PHRASE is what a stranger types when they have the problem
  and do not know your framework exists.

  AUTHOR TERMS (nobody types these — they were invented inside your
  head):
    "prompt orchestration methodology"
    "AI leverage architecture"

  BUYER PHRASES (typed by people with a card out and a problem they
  can name):
    "how to publish a book without hiring a writer"
    "make money with AI without coding"

THE SLOTS

  SLOT 1: ______________________________________________________
  SLOT 2: ______________________________________________________
  SLOT 3: ______________________________________________________
  SLOT 4: ______________________________________________________
  SLOT 5: ______________________________________________________
  SLOT 6: ______________________________________________________
  SLOT 7: ______________________________________________________

THE TEST — apply to every slot, the same way every time:

  "Would a person who has never heard of me type this into a search
  bar, or would only a person who already follows me type it?"

  If the second: delete it and write a phrase a beginner would type.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
LISTING-LEVEL DECISIONS THAT SIT NEXT TO THE METADATA
---------------------------------------------------------------------

These are not metadata fields, but they are decided at the same time
and they contradict the metadata if you do them last.

KDP SELECT — decide explicitly, in writing
  The program trades exclusivity for a discovery channel. The
  exclusivity is real, and it is the whole cost.

  Enroll when ALL THREE are true:
    [ ] This is your first product
    [ ] You have no audience of your own yet
    [ ] You have no other marketplace plan in motion

  Go wide when ANY ONE is true:
    [ ] Your sales are already concentrated outside Amazon
    [ ] You sell directly
    [ ] Your product is a workbook or bundle that buyers expect in a
        format the channel handles poorly

  My decision:      ______________________________
  The condition I am trading against:
  ______________________________________________________________

  The wrong move is enrolling by default because it was on the setup
  screen. Defaults are how people give away optionality they later
  need.

DEPLOYMENT — two marketplaces, then wait
  [ ] Deployed to exactly two largest English-language marketplaces
  [ ] Thirty-day wait started on: ____ / ____ / ________
  [ ] Calendar reminder set to review where sales actually landed
  [ ] No expansion into a language you cannot support

  Two gives you a comparison; five gives you five sets of tax and
  payment forms, five metadata fields to correct, and no clean data.

  A translated listing with English customer support and an
  English-only product produces refunds and one-star reviews in a
  language you cannot read. Machine translation of a description is
  not localisation, and buyers can tell within one paragraph.
