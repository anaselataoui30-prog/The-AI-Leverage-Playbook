The AI Leverage Playbook — Chapter 5

HOW TO USE THIS FILE

A published product with no traffic is a warehouse with no road. The
inventory is fine. Nothing reaches it.

Borrowed traffic is rented, not owned. A follower count on a platform
you do not control is rented space. The rent can be raised, the terms
changed, or the lease ended, and none of those decisions require your
consent. Building the entire road on rented land means one policy
change can put you back at zero with the product still listed and
nobody arriving.

The minimum owned layer is three pieces: one landing page, one email
capture, one sequence of three emails. That is the whole thing. Not
a funnel diagram with eleven boxes and a quiz.

headlines and you have not chosen.

  ______________________________________________________________

  [ ] All three lines present

---------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 3 — THE SINGLE CAPTURE FIELD
---------------------------------------------------------------------

One field. Email only.

  [ EMAIL ADDRESS                        ]  [ Send it to me ]

  [ ] Exactly one input field
  [ ] No phone number — on a nonfiction page it signals a sales
      call nobody asked for
  [ ] No first name, no company, no "how did you hear about us"
  [ ] The button names what happens next, not "Submit"

Every additional field lowers the share of people who finish the
form. The landing page has one job: convert an arriving stranger
into an email address. The capture is the asset. The sequence is
what turns it into revenue.

WHAT LANDS IN THE INBOX
  The first email of the three-email sequence (Asset 10). Write its
  subject line here so the confirmation block can name it:

  Subject: ______________________________________________________

  [ ] The subject line in this block matches the actual Email 1
      subject, character for character
  [ ] Email 1 delivers and asks for nothing


---------------------------------------------------------------------
THE PAGE, IN ORDER
---------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. Headline — the change of state
  2. Three-line description — outcome, audience, timeframe + obstacle
  3. Single email field + a button that names the next step
  4. Delivery confirmation — what happens now, and what to look for

Nothing else. No testimonials you have not earned, no countdown
timer, no "as featured in." A page with one job converts better than
a page with five, because a page with five turns the visitor into a
decision-maker at the moment they have the least patience for it.

WHERE THE TRAFFIC COMES FROM (routing reminder)
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Chapter 2's repurposing prompt produces five asset types from one
edited draft. Each belongs somewhere specific, and each needs a
route back to the product page that does not depend on the viewer's
memory.

  ASSET TYPE      PLATFORM                 ROUTING
  Thread          text-first social        final post names the
                                           destination, or pinned
                                           reply carries it
  Long post       professional network     link at the end of the
                                           post body
  Email           owned list               direct link in the body
  Short script    video, long-form capable pinned comment plus a
                                           link in the channel bio
  Caption         visual-first social      link in bio

Two rules make the table work:

  - One destination per asset, not three. A post with three links
    converts like a post with none.
  - No asset routes to another social post. A route that ends on a
    platform you do not own is not a route.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
THE FOUR COMPONENTS OF EVERY SHORT POST
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Whatever routes traffic to this page, each post carries four parts
in order. Skip one and the post either gets no attention or gets
attention and routes it nowhere.

  1. HOOK — first three seconds
     Names a problem or an outcome specific enough that the right
     person stops. No greeting. No "in this video." No describing
     what you are about to describe.

  2. MECHANISM — next fifteen to twenty seconds
     The actual mechanism, named and shown, not summarised. "Here
     are three tips" is not a mechanism. "The tables come before the
     prompts, because prompts with no rows to run against are
     untestable" is a mechanism.

  3. PROOF — following twenty seconds
     What the mechanism produced. A number, a before-and-after, a
     screenshot of a table. Proof is not a claim about your
     expertise, it is evidence that the mechanism ran.

  4. NEXT STEP — final five seconds
     One action, one destination. "Follow for more" is not a next
     step, it is a request to stay in the same place. The close
     names where to go and what is there.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
THE HOOK REWRITE — REFERENCE
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Weak:   "Here is how I use AI to write books faster than ever."

Strong: "You can ship a 40-page product in 30 days without writing a
         draft. The pipeline that does it has four stages, and the
         third one is where everyone skips the work."

Why: the weak hook promises speed to an audience that already
believes speed is possible, which makes it noise. The strong hook
names a format, a timeframe, and an obstacle, and the last clause
tells the viewer there is a specific failure mode they are probably
committing. A person in that situation recognises themselves in
under two seconds.
