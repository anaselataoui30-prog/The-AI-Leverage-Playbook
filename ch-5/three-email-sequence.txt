HOW TO USE THIS FILE

Three emails, in this order, each with one job. The sequence is
short on purpose. Long sequences exist to wear people down, and
wearing people down produces refunds and unsubscribes rather than
buyers.

Every claim, number, and name in these emails traces to a grounded
Ideas row. If it does not, cut it. If the proof you need does not
exist yet, write [NEEDS INPUT: what is missing] rather than
inventing one.

THE SEQUENCE AT A GLANCE
---------------------------------------------------------------------

  #   JOB        WORDS      SUBJECT EXAMPLE
  1   Delivery   80–120     "Your four-table base is ready"
  2   Proof      150–250    "One brief, nine assets, forty minutes"
  3   Ask        100–150    "The full pipeline, if you want it"

  Total: under 520 words for the entire sequence.

Short on purpose. A long sequence gives the reader more
opportunities to decide you are not worth opening, and the decision
is made in the first email regardless.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
THE THREE FAILURES
---------------------------------------------------------------------

1. ASKING IN EMAIL 1
   The most common and the most expensive. It converts slightly
   better on day one and poisons every email after it.

2. PROOF WITHOUT A NUMBER
   "It worked really well for me" is not proof, it is a claim about
   your expertise. A number, a before-and-after, or a screenshot is
   proof that the mechanism ran.

3. TWO LINKS IN EMAIL 3
   One destination per asset. A post or an email with three links
   converts like one with none.
