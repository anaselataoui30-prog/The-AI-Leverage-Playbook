=====================================================================
ASSET 5 — PRODUCT PROMISE WORKSHEET
The AI Leverage Playbook — Chapter 3
=====================================================================

HOW TO USE THIS FILE

One page, four slots, plus three rewrite prompts. Write the promise
sentence before the outline, and rewrite it after every pass.

A topic is not a product. A topic describes territory. A promise
describes a change of state. Territory cannot be failed, which is
why nobody buys it.

"A guide to using AI for content" is a topic. It cannot be graded.
A reader who buys it has no way to know whether you delivered, and a
reader with no way to check does not buy in the first place.


========================= START COPYING HERE =========================

THE FORMULA

  This [format] helps [specific audience] achieve [specific outcome]
  in [timeframe] without [specific obstacle].

FILLED EXAMPLE

  This 40-page playbook helps non-technical solo operators publish
  one grounded chapter per day in 30 days without hiring a writer or
  writing a line of code.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
SLOT 1 — FORMAT
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Length and shape. A number and a noun. Not "a comprehensive guide."

  This __________________________________________________________

  Prompts:
  - How many pages, words, or modules? Write the number.
  - What shape is it — playbook, workbook, checklist, template pack?
  - Would a buyer know from this slot alone how long it takes to
    finish?


---------------------------------------------------------------------
SLOT 2 — SPECIFIC AUDIENCE
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Narrow enough that a specific person recognises themselves. If
everyone is the audience, no one is.

  helps _________________________________________________________

  Prompts:
  - Name the person and the situation they are already in.
  - What have they already bought that did not work? Say it.
  - Who is explicitly NOT in this audience? The exclusion is how a
    promise becomes believable to the person it was written for.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
SLOT 3 — SPECIFIC OUTCOME
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Observable, not aspirational. A reader must be able to tell, on the
last page, whether they got it.

  achieve _______________________________________________________

  Prompts:
  - Can the reader check this at the end? If not, it is a feeling,
    not an outcome.
  - Is it something they produce or something they become?
    "Publish one chapter a day" is checkable. "Become an AI power
    user" is not.
  - Would a stranger be able to verify it by asking them one
    question?


---------------------------------------------------------------------
SLOT 4 — TIMEFRAME
---------------------------------------------------------------------

A number. Not "fast," not "quickly," not "in no time."

  in ____________________________________________________________

  Prompts:
  - Is the number honest for the audience in slot 2?
  - Is it a build timeframe or a revenue timeframe? They are not
    the same, and confusing them produces refund requests.
  - What exactly is finished at the end of it — the system, the
    product, or the income?


---------------------------------------------------------------------
SLOT 5 — SPECIFIC OBSTACLE
---------------------------------------------------------------------

The thing they already tried and failed at. This is the only reason
they are reading instead of writing.

  without _______________________________________________________

  Prompts:
  - What have they already tried that failed?
  - What does this product let them skip — a skill, a hire, a tool,
    a step?
  - Does this slot exclude somebody? It should. An exclusion is not
    a weakness in the promise.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
ASSEMBLED PROMISE
---------------------------------------------------------------------

  This __________________________________ helps ____________________
  achieve _______________________________ in ______________________
  without ______________________________________________________.

Read it aloud. If you stumble, the slots are too long or one of
them is doing two jobs. Split it.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
THE FOUR-SLOT CHECK
---------------------------------------------------------------------

All four mandatory, none negotiable, or the sentence is not a
promise:

  [ ] Format is a length and a shape
  [ ] Audience is narrow enough that a specific person recognises
      themselves
  [ ] Outcome is observable, not aspirational
  [ ] Timeframe is a number
  [ ] Obstacle is the thing they already tried and failed at

The obstacle slot does the most work and gets skipped the most.
"Without writing code" excludes people. Good.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
THREE REWRITE PROMPTS
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Use these when a slot feels true but unverifiable. Each one attacks
a different failure.

REWRITE PROMPT 1 — IS THE OUTCOME OBSERVABLE?
  Ask: "How would I know, on the last page, whether the reader got
  this?"
  If the answer is a feeling, a mindset, or an understanding, the
  outcome is aspirational. Rewrite it as something they produce,
  file, publish, or can show someone.

REWRITE PROMPT 2 — IS THE OBSTACLE REAL?
  Ask: "Name a person I know who tried this and failed at exactly
  this step."
  If no such person comes to mind, the obstacle is invented. Go back
  to a customer conversation or your own failed attempt and take the
  obstacle from there. Invented obstacles produce promises nobody
  was waiting for.

REWRITE PROMPT 3 — IS THE AUDIENCE TOO WIDE?
  Ask: "Who is this explicitly NOT for?"
  Write the exclusion down. If you cannot name someone the promise
  excludes, the audience is "everyone," which means the promise
  belongs to no one. Narrow it until it excludes a real group, then
  check that the group you kept is the one with the card out.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
WHEN THE PROMISE IS FINISHED
---------------------------------------------------------------------

The promise is not decoration. It does three jobs downstream and you
will use it again in every chapter:

  1. STRUCTURE — Pass one of the five passes deletes any chapter
     that does not prove the promise, regardless of how good the
     draft is.
  2. LISTING — The three-line description in Chapter 4 is this
     promise, split at the slots.
  3. LANDING PAGE — The headline in Chapter 5 is this promise,
     compressed to a line.

Rewrite it after every structuring pass. A promise that never
changes was never tested against the product.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
BUILD SESSION — FROM CHAPTER 3
---------------------------------------------------------------------

  [ ] Write the promise sentence using all four slots, then read it
      aloud and confirm the obstacle names something a real reader
      already tried and failed at
  [ ] Run pass one on your chapter list and delete every chapter that
      does not prove the promise, even if the draft is good
  [ ] Reorder the surviving chapters by action sequence, naming what
      the reader has produced by the end of each one
  [ ] Attach a checkable source to every number and named framework,
      and cut or ground every claim without one
  [ ] Run the three compression cuts until the manuscript lands
      between 70 and 80 percent of its draft length
  [ ] Package it: thumbnail-legible cover, title naming the outcome,
      subtitle naming audience, timeframe and obstacle, consistent
      interior headings, and a one-action final page
